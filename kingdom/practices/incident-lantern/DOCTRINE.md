# Incident Lantern doctrine

## The first proposition

Security is not only the absence of a successful exploit. It is the ability to
make an adverse or uncertain event visible, bounded, understandable, and easy
to answer without inventing certainty or widening harm.

An opaque control can stop one event and still leave a fragile system. A useful
incident practice shortens four paths:

```text
discover the bounded event
    -> understand what is known and unknown
    -> choose a proportionate, authorized response
    -> preserve a reviewed lesson for the next design
```

Incident Lantern serves those paths locally. It does not observe live systems
or perform the response.

## Discoverable: bind the source before telling the story

An incident explanation begins with a source contract, not a narrative. The
accepted source is one closed Future-KARMA categorical event and one claimed
receipt. The pinned planner is freshly replayed, the claimed receipt must match
that replay, and the supplied receipt object is then discarded.

This gives the explanation a reproducible computational basis. It does not
give the event authentic external provenance. The receipt has no trustworthy
event clock, recurrence history, deduplication state, or live blast radius, so
the timeline names only the five contract phases:

1. categorical observation received;
2. source bindings validated;
3. receipt replayed;
4. policy decision produced or boundary halted;
5. human review remains pending.

No timestamp is better than a fabricated timestamp. No causal claim is better
than a smooth but unsupported story.

## Understandable: facts, inferences, and unknowns do not share a costume

The three epistemic lanes are separate data structures and separate visual
lanes.

**Facts** describe what the bounded machinery established: the categorical
contract was accepted, the source declared offline scope and no authority, the
receipt survived exact replay, and the pinned policy produced a proposal
without executing it. “Confirmed” belongs to those statements about the local
computation; it does not make the source a confirmed real-world attack.

**Inferences** describe the pinned policy's reading. A mapped threat family is
a policy-derived classification, not an identity, intention, moral verdict, or
reputation claim. The action posture is decision support, not evidence that a
containment change happened.

**Unknowns** remain visible until evidence outside this contract resolves
them. They include external provenance, identity and intent, live impact,
event time and sequence, actual containment state, and caller-owned retention.
Unknown is a result. It is never an invitation to fill the gap with confidence
language.

## A valid halt is information

A replay-verified Future-KARMA halt is not malformed input. Hiding it would
erase precisely the uncertainty an incident practice should expose.

Incident Lantern therefore renders it as `halted-for-review`, with a boundary
halt, a quarantine proposal, an additional unknown for the withheld selector,
and a regression candidate that is `blocked-until-mapped`.

The event's digest remains available for correlation inside an authorized
boundary. Its unreviewed surface, mechanism, and signal do not enter the
incident or downloaded candidate. They become fixed `withheld-unreviewed`
placeholders. The distinction is deliberate:

- **valid but unmapped** becomes visible without reflection;
- **tampered or unverifiable** is rejected without output.

Discoverability does not require putting private or attacker-influenced
material on display.

## Actionable: a quick answer still needs authority and a way back

An action card is useful only when it answers more than “what should we do?”
Each card must also expose:

- who must authorize it;
- whether anything happens automatically;
- its current actual effect;
- its expected blast-radius posture;
- what must be checked before acting;
- whether and how it can be reversed;
- how success and collateral change would be verified.

Incident Lantern's cards are ranked proposals. Their authority is
`human-required`, their state is `not-executed`, their automatic flag is false,
and their actual effect is none. A rollback sentence is a review requirement,
not proof that a future live change is reversible. “Conditional” remains
conditional until an owner tests the concrete path.

No event content, receipt, digest, threat label, candidate, dashboard, or
urgency can mint authority. Authorization must come from the owner of the
affected boundary under a separate live change process.

## Learnable: preserve the lesson without silently changing the learner

The regression candidate is a handoff to human review, not an automatic
feedback loop. It can make a future fixture easier to author and compare while
keeping the current classifier still.

A candidate carries:

- source and policy bindings;
- a minimized event projection;
- the current replayed expectation;
- a canonical digest;
- an explicit promotion state and nonclaims.

It does not edit the policy, threat model, corpus, pins, tests, or deployment.
Promotion requires those artifacts to be reviewed together because changing
only a rule can create collisions, weaken defaults, or turn a remembered edge
case into an unexamined capability.

The browser's Download button is a narrow exception to the otherwise
zero-write workflow: an explicit human click may create one local candidate
file. The object URL is temporary, but the downloaded file is real and remains
under the user's retention choices. Download is not installation, adoption,
publication, or execution.

## Privacy is an architectural boundary

Raw traffic belongs upstream of this practice, inside a separately authorized
and reviewed adapter. Incident Lantern accepts only the closed categorical
Future-KARMA event. It has no field for request bodies, headers, prompts,
credentials, paths, identity, or free-form attacker text.

Minimization is not anonymity proof. Digests and categorical combinations can
still be sensitive in context, so access, retention, audience, correlation,
and deletion remain caller responsibilities. Public availability, technical
readability, or a playful presentation never implies consent to identify,
attribute, advertise with, shame, contact, or retaliate against anyone.

## The dashboard is a reader, not a responder

The local dashboard validates canonical shape and digests, pins the reviewed
engine and catalogs, checks planned selectors against the reviewed policy,
resolves every evidence reference, and reconstructs the expected explanation
before constructing its view with text nodes. It separates facts, inferences,
unknowns, action cards, and learning guidance so a responder can scan the
incident without losing the evidence boundary.

Its local JavaScript may read one chosen file, hold the validated incident in
page memory, clear that state, and download the embedded candidate after an
explicit click. It has no network request, storage, cookie, analytics,
clipboard, messaging, model, command, or infrastructure-control path.

Browser validation does not freshly run Future-KARMA and does not authenticate
where a file came from. Use the Python `verify` command when the source wrapper
is available. The page keeps this warning visible even after a successful
self-check. The dashboard is the legibility layer over that verified artifact,
not a substitute for the replay boundary.

## The Lantern loop

The reusable loop is:

1. **Observe** through an authorized adapter that emits only a bounded
   categorical event.
2. **Explain** through fresh replay, minimized bindings, and an explicit
   knowledge map.
3. **Decide** with proposal cards that name authority, scope, rollback, and
   verification.
4. **Act** only through a separate authorized owner and change process.
5. **Verify** the actual environment; never infer containment from the card.
6. **Learn** by reviewing a candidate against policy, threat model, corpus,
   pins, and tests.
7. **Harden** only after regression and collision checks pay the new
   verification debt.

The loop should reduce time to understanding and safe decision, not reward
speed without evidence.

## Nonclaims and evidence

The implementation's zero controls state that this bounded engine made no
network call, process spawn, model call, secret read, filesystem write,
external message, authority grant, action execution, classifier mutation, or
retained record. Those are properties of the reviewed code path and its tests,
not claims about every caller, browser, operating system, or future adapter.

Content digests prove deterministic self-consistency, not authentic origin.
Closed schemas reduce ambiguity, not operational risk by themselves. A passing
macOS `sandbox-exec` rehearsal is evidence for that invocation and profile,
not proof of general Darwin confinement. Automated accessibility structure is
not a substitute for manual Safari, keyboard, zoom, and VoiceOver review.

Incident Lantern is therefore a local, offline explanation and learning
practice. It claims no production integration, live telemetry, comprehensive
coverage, live efficacy, identity, intent, guilt, impact, legal status, or
authority to act.
