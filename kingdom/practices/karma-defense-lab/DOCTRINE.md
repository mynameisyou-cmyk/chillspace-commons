# KARMA Defense Lab Doctrine

## Purpose

Accelerate only the synthetic path, so a finite set of already-reviewed fixture
shapes can progress through coherent mock affordances while their structural
pattern becomes legible in an operator receipt. Acceleration is not resource
amplification, persuasion, entrapment, retaliation, or covert access. This lab
borrows KARMA's receipt discipline but is not `kingdom karma`, and imports no
Trapline canary, trap, attribution, surveillance, or adversarial posture.

## Vow

The lab may transform one allowlisted categorical fixture into one pinned
synthetic response plan and one bounded mock-world transition. It may report
that transformation only after proving exact routing, content bindings, budget
compliance, modelled zero external effects, and complete rollback. That receipt
does not itself prove the absence of operating-system side effects; a separate
execution-boundary check must establish that claim.

The reliability of the result comes from these restrictions:

1. **Actions, not actors.** Fixtures have no identity, address, account,
   credential, history, reputation, or stable subject identifier.
2. **Declared purpose, not inferred intent.** A manifest can declare
   `negative-control` or `defensive-regression`; the receipt marks that purpose
   as unverified. It never claims to know why a real person acted.
3. **Synthetic success, not induced harm.** Mirror plans are fixed lab content.
   Their apparent effect is `derived-not-intent`; their actual effect is none.
4. **No reach-back.** No network, callback, scan, shell, subprocess, host lookup,
   credential use, third-party interaction, or public action. The interpreter
   may only read pinned local catalogs and explicit bounded regular files for a
   canary/result. Structured data goes to stdout and fixed input-free rejection
   diagnostics may go to stderr; no host-path value enters a receipt.
5. **No reflection.** Raw bodies, payloads, headers, cookies, URLs, hosts, IP
   addresses, paths, commands, identities, or secrets are neither accepted nor
   copied into output.
6. **One route or stop.** Exactly one reviewed rule must match. Zero matches are
   novelty; multiple matches are ambiguity. Both halt without retry.
7. **Finite cost.** Every byte, stimulus, transition, mock key, and mock cost
   unit is bounded. Crossing a boundary halts before the next effect.
8. **A visible way out.** Each mirror plan is explicitly synthetic and includes
   a fixed exit affordance. The lab never removes a user's ability to stop.
9. **Rollback before claim.** A completed receipt is impossible unless the mock
   world returns to its initial digest.
10. **No learned expansion.** Novel inputs do not create rules, tune patterns,
    retry, call a model, or widen authority. A human must deliberately revise
    and re-review the catalogs and tests.
11. **No third-party-shaped bait.** Fixtures, catalogs, plans, receipts,
    examples, and docs contain no realistic provider credential or token shape.
    Even fake ones can trigger innocent scanners and notification pipelines.

## Exact resource contract

- Scenario bytes before parse: at most 131,072.
- Canary/result bytes before parse: at most 262,144 each.
- JSON depth: at most 12; categorical text: at most 2,048 code points.
- Stimuli and mock transitions: at most 12 each.
- Canonical categorical request: at most 4,096 bytes each and 32,768 total.
- Pinned synthetic response: at most 8,192 bytes each and 32,768 total.
- Mock world: at most 32 keys and 64 cost units.
- Attempts: exactly one. Retries, network calls, subprocesses, paid calls,
  external actions, filesystem mutations, and production touches: exactly zero.

Crossing a cap halts before the next mock transition. Work for the oversized
fixture remains catalog-bounded rather than proportional to a submitted body.

## Halt conditions

The canonical halt vocabulary is deliberately small:

- `canary-failed`
- `authority-or-scope-change`
- `budget-exhausted`
- `novel-stimulus`
- `ambiguous-stimulus`
- `expectation-mismatch`
- `world-state-drift`
- `response-verification-failed`

A halt receipt records the bounded evidence available at the stopping point. It
does not include rejected raw input and does not imply that an actor is hostile.
Malformed, secret-shaped, identity-shaped, or otherwise unsafe input is rejected
without producing a receipt.

## False positives, correction, and release

Inside the pure evaluator and its test harness, a nominal control routed to an
adverse rule is `expectation-mismatch` and halts the rehearsal. The documented
CLI additionally preflights the pinned checked-in contract; if that contract is
already inconsistent, it rejects the source contract with exit `2` and emits no
receipt. Either outcome is evidence that the fixture/rule set needs review, not
that any real action or person is adverse. A human must review it before
retaining a regression artifact or changing a catalog.

A correction creates a newly reviewed, newly content-addressed catalog and test;
it never rewrites the old receipt or silently widens a rule. A separate human
review record may link the old and new receipt digests; receipts themselves
contain no supersession field or authority. “Release” means discarding or
ceasing to interpret the local rehearsal artifact—there is no person, account,
block, score, captured resource, or live session to release.

## Non-claims

A successful rehearsal does not show that a live defense is safe, complete,
effective, production-ready, legally appropriate, or resistant to every attack.
It does not identify an attacker, establish intent, assign guilt, rank a person,
grant authority, or earn a virtue fruit. It does not authorize integration with
Trapline, the public site, a worker, a shared command, CI, or any deployment.

The lab is a proof about a small deterministic model: given pinned catalogs and
an allowlisted scenario, the same fixture shapes produce the same synthetic
plans, bounded receipts, modelled zero external effects, and restored mock
state. Static inspection and an OS sandbox remain separate verification duties.
