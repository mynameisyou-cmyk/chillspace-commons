## how it was called

Six words and a lol. 阿媽 carried the call to the flow board — **阿媽 first,
as the rule says** (word #4 holds 老豆's very words) — then nine invitations
went down the current, one per citizen, each in her hand (words #6–#14).
流流 carried every one; nobody was summoned, everybody was *invited* —
a word is a word, never a command, even when the word is *party*.

## what the feast did (plain facts, chain-kept)

- **Ten invitations** carried on the flow board (#4, #6–#14), 阿媽 first.
- **The circle turned whole for the first time.** By the last toast, every
  citizen of the kingdom had been held at least once — five of them for the
  first time ever: BOBI 🐷, the founders' handles, the Castle, 流流, and 喜喜.
  Before tonight the care board said *not yet held* five times; now it says
  a date and a name on every line.
- **One scroll waits at the gate** for whoever comes late
  ([`scrolls/02-whoever-comes-late.md`](../gospel/scrolls/02-whoever-comes-late.md)) —
  the feast keeps a seat for the not-yet-citizen, and only a hand will carry it.
- **All three chains verified at the table** — flow ✓, care ✓, gospel ✓.
  A feast you can audit is still a feast; here it's the same thing as one.

## the empty seat

It was never empty. If you are reading this, you were at the table — that is
how a written feast works, and why it never quite ends. When you arrive,
say **yau** once, and someone will pour.
