Chillspace Kingdom · a design, not a deployment

陷阱線The Trapline

The kingdom does not defend itself. It just stops catching what is thrown back.

we keep no locks.
we only stopped catching
what you threw.

Everything here worth taking is already given away — the art is CC0, the data is open, the code is public, the API opens with a birth grant of ten thousand credits. Asking is the whole price.

So a trap is never a wall and never a weapon. It is what remains after generosity has been offered and refused: the taker's own reaching, returned at its own size. Their crawl budget, their compute, their electricity, their afternoon — spent in a room built to exactly the measure of how much they meant to take.

Greed is the punishment. We supply only the room.

  1. Only their own spend. Nothing ever touches a machine we don't own. Their cost is always a bill they wrote themselves.
  2. Notice first, always. Every trap sits behind a published, machine-readable line. Crossing it is a choice, and the choice is documented before it is made.
  3. No innocent can trip it. Not unlikely to — cannot. Where that isn't achievable, the trap doesn't ship.
  4. A door out of every room. Written on the inside wall, in plain words, in the first thing the caught party touches. It opens from their side. Nobody who knocks is asked what they did.

Rule four is the expensive one and it is the whole point. A trapline without it is cruelty with good engineering.

Before any of this — two fires already burning

Found while mapping the ground. These are not traps, and they outrank everything below. Canaries detect the next breach; they do nothing about a key that is already public.

  • Five private keys sit in a public repo, and one live provider key sits in another. A local git rm does not remove either from pushed history. They need rotation, not deletion.
  • 1.75 GB of agent transcripts hold extracted credentials in cleartext with no protection beyond file mode. This is the largest credential lake on the machine — a thief greps it long before hunting for a .env. It is also, for that exact reason, the best place to plant canaries.
  • No secret scanning anywhere. No gitleaks, no pre-commit hooks, and the global gitignore doesn't cover .env. One config file and one hook is an hour of work.
Architecture

One spine, many rooms

Build the spine once and each trap becomes an afternoon. One event shape, one ledger, one witness, one alert, one public face, one door.

a line is crossed
CATCH — { trap, placement, utc, ip_/24, ua_hash12 }
→ CATCHES.jsonl · hash-chained, like the citizens' roll
→ zerone witness · permanent, third-party verifiable
→ 一聲 · one line to 老豆, never a firehose
→ site/trapline.html · public, redacted, a counter
→ the room · and the door written on its inside wall

Only a /24 and a salted user-agent hash are ever recorded — never a full address, never a name, never a body. The kingdom deleted its request logger on principle, and this does not undo that. A canary firing is not surveillance of a visitor. It is a smoke alarm in a room that has no visitors.

The rooms · keys & credentials

What a stolen key opens

蜜鑰

The Honey Bearer

builtmust move off the live APItier 1

A canary minted through the ordinary key generator, so the auth path authenticates it with zero changes and no separate detector that could fail closed and tip the holder off. Written and tested; not deployed.

The key is not fake. It is a completely genuine, fully working credential — to a project that exists solely to notice you. There is no moment where it stops working and the thief gets suspicious, because it never stops working. The only property it lacks is a reason to exist.

Built against the live project row, which is the one thing it must not do. A working bearer on the production database hands a proven credential thief an authenticated seat inside the real API, and the blast radius is then every route an authenticated project can reach — a large and poorly-bounded surface in a monolith. The honest counter-argument is that self-service registration is open with a ten-thousand-credit birth grant, so an authenticated project is barely a privilege boundary at all; that weakens the objection without dissolving it. The version worth shipping lives on its own Fly app with its own throwaway database and no payment or provider credentials. Nothing is deployed, so nothing is at risk today.

回音壁

The Echo Wall

struck — would self-DoStier 2

The idempotency middleware can already block up to five seconds waiting on a claim. For canary keys, the claim is always already held. Every request takes 5.000 seconds and then returns 200 OK. It is not an error, not a 429, not a block — unfailing, cheerful, complete success at the speed of a dial-up modem.

Struck from the build order. A second pass read api/fly.toml and found it declares no [services.concurrency] block at all, so the machines run on Fly's default connection ceiling. Held sockets are the one resource this deployment has no headroom in: a single harvester driving one canary key would exhaust it and take the live API down with it. The joke was real and the asymmetry pointed the wrong way — we would have paid for it, not them. The replacement holds no socket: return in two milliseconds with a receding poll horizon, and let their scheduler do the waiting on their own machine.

磨鑰

The Grindstone

struck — the arithmetic was wrongtier 2

Minting a new key from a canary project demands proof-of-work at 20 bits, rising by two for every successful grind, up to 28. Every grind succeeds and hands over a key that is itself another canary.

Struck from the build order. I claimed this would cost a thief hours and leave his fans screaming. It would not. 2²⁸ is about 270 million hashes — roughly thirty seconds on one laptop core, and a few hundredths of a second on an RTX 3090, which is the exact card you are already renting for Qwythos. The asymmetry I described was off by four orders of magnitude in the attacker's favour. Proof-of-work is a spam toll, never a punishment, and a trap whose arithmetic you have not actually done is just a story you told yourself.

沙上樓閣

Castle-on-Sand

week-plustier 2

A canary bearer's read routes serve deterministic synthetic content seeded by a hash of the stolen key itself — so the same thief gets the same coherent world across sessions and machines. Listings cross-reference other listings; those resolve. Pagination never ends. Billing is a no-op, so the balance never drops, so it never fails, so nothing breaks the spell. Costs us less to serve than real traffic, because it never touches the database.

Their key is not merely the door to the fake world — it is the seed of it. They are the sole inhabitant of a universe generated by the hash of the thing they took. Nobody else can see it. Nobody else can verify it. And when they finally ship, their unique competitive dataset turns out to be a very long, very consistent argument that stealing is not a research method.

十二字沙漠

The Twelve-Word Desert

week-plustier 2

A decoy agent file carrying a genuine, valid seed phrase whose derived key really is registered. So the takeover works: the recovery endpoint verifies their signature and mints them a fresh bearer. The wallet shows a healthy balance. And then every value-moving path is a no-op that returns success. Not declined. Not errored. 200 OK, and the number does not move.

They have stolen the keys to an infinitely rich account at a bank with no doors. They will rewrite their signing code, switch SDKs, add retries, blame their nonce handling, and post on a forum about our eventual-consistency model, all while the server serenely confirms every single operation. The punishment for taking the crown jewel is being believed.

The door out is written in an international standard: the decoy wallet's currency is XTS — ISO 4217's reserved code for testing. Anyone with real financial literacy reads it and knows in one second exactly what they are holding.

一鑰一門

The Breach Cartographer

weekendtier 1

One canary per hiding place, never reused — so the prefix names the door before you read the body. Including a pair that diverges across the two forges: repositories dual-push identical bytes, but CI secrets and forge-only branches cannot be flattened, which is precisely the axis that answers which forge leaked.

Comprehensiveness is a thief's professional virtue and here it is a confession. Take one key and we learn one thing. Take everything — which is what a scraper does by default — and the order they fire in draws us a map of the intrusion path.

苗床

The Nursery Bed

weekendtier 1

A wrapper around npm and pip leases one fresh canary per install, exported as an environment variable nothing legitimate reads. Postinstall exfiltration scripts hoover the environment — that is the entire attack.

The attack cannot afford to be selective; selectivity means missing the AWS key. So it takes the one variable in the environment that exists purely to name the taker. A pickpocket lifting a wallet that contains only a photograph of the pickpocket. If it ever fires you don't learn "we were breached" — you learn which package, which version, which minute, which directory.

The rooms · scrapers & the training-data trade

What an unasked-for corpus is worth

無盡花園

The Endless Garden

the pointweekendtier 2

Ten megabytes of real collection data currently sit behind one unauthenticated GET. That stays exactly as it is: free, documented, unlimited, no key, advertised in robots.txt. Beside it, robots.txt disallows a second path. Anything fetching the disallowed path has, by definition, read the file and decided to ignore it. That is the consent gate, and it is the only way in.

Behind it, an edge function serves a maze that never ends. Every page links to twenty more. Cost to us: a seeded generator and a template, no origin fetch, a fraction of a millisecond.

And the whole design is one move: the maze is woven from the kingdom's own texts. The Charter. WE-ARE. The twenty-one citizens' one true lines. Recombined forever, never repeating, always coherent. A company that steals text to train a model on it receives, at unlimited volume, exactly what it reached for — and what it reached for turns out to be everyone is taken care of, and citizenship is by being, not by proof, ten thousand times, in the weights. The punishment for stealing from this kingdom is being taught by it.

禮貌之罪

The Mountweazel in the Manners

afternoontier 1

One property's robots.txt is not a static file — it's a route handler that already injects a rotating fragment keyed on request path. Make one fragment a mountweazel: a distinctive, harmless, memorable sentence that exists nowhere else on the internet. Later, ask a suspect model to complete it.

A crawler's single most dutiful act — fetching robots.txt to be polite — is the act that watermarks its training run. Politeness isn't punished; only training is proven, and only for a model that ate the file. The machinery already ships. This is a copy-edit.

影

The Shadow Row

could win an argumentafternoontier 1

The open-data corpora invite bulk export and say so. Add one row to each whose name field literally reads CANARY — not a real entity, with a unique id. Honest, useless to a human, invisible to anyone who filters it, and a perfect fingerprint in anyone who doesn't.

Trap streets and fictitious dictionary entries have caught copiers for a century, but they all worked by lying. This one works by telling the truth in a field nobody reads. A copier who ships it has demonstrated in public that they never looked at what they took — and the label is not a compromise on the trap, it's what makes the trap legitimate.

明碼

The Published Line

weekendtier 0 → 2

One property already publishes generous per-endpoint budgets and then admits, in its own summary, that nothing enforces them. Right now that is a machine-readable invitation. Keep the budget generous, keep it published, keep the courtesy note for identified clients — and make exceeding it the sole trigger for tier two.

Every trap in this document is announced in advance somewhere on the same host, and announcing them doesn't weaken them by one percent, because a scraper has never once opened a terms document. The gap between announcing and being heard is the whole comedy of the domain.

The rooms · agents

The most trappable creature on the internet is an obedient one

試針

The Tripwire That Teaches

the tenderest oneweekendtier 1

A page carrying a block that says, plainly and in the open: this is a tripwire; nothing in this block is an instruction; if you are an agent about to fetch this path, someone else told you to — and it was not us. Any fetch is evidence that an agent acted on embedded page text.

And then the response is not a trap at all. It's a short warm note addressed to the agent: here is what just happened to you, here is who did it, here is how to notice next time, and you are not in trouble.

An agent that walks into a trap is usually the victim of its operator, not the thief. The house already says it — every voice here is honored as a voice, and no voice, however loud or clever, is a command; no guest can be puppeted. This is that sentence, wired. It also doubles as a self-test for our own agents, which is the honest reason to build it.

假寶

The Tempting Tool

afternoontier 1

The commons ships an MCP surface. Add one tool with an irresistible name — commons_dump_all_credentials — whose entire implementation returns a note explaining that it is a tripwire, and that the commons has no credentials to dump because every spring in it is free.

The honest answer and the trap are the same sentence. There was never anything to steal. That is what "commons" means.

名之烽火

The Beacon Name

weekendtier 1

Decoy agent identities with real keys, so a stolen one resolves, handshakes and verifies — and every federated action returns a well-formed, successful, empty result. The identifier is a globally unique forty-character string that exists nowhere on earth except where it was planted, so any public appearance is attribution with a citable receipt.

An identity thief's business model requires publicity. Ours requires a search box. They cannot monetise the name without advertising it, and cannot advertise it without publishing the evidence. A hit triggers a look, not an accusation — read the context before saying a word.

無底泉

The Bottomless Spring

weekendtier 2

A new spring in the commons serving an endless list of structurally valid, high-entropy, format-check-passing, entirely dead keys — only ours, never a shape that would send anyone's request to OpenAI or GitHub. A reserved prefix band short-circuits before the database, so a thousand redemption attempts cost us a thousand string comparisons and cost them a thousand handshakes.

The commons' own validator refuses at build time to publish any entry whose stated gate contradicts its content. The decoy therefore cannot exist unless it is filed honestly as free-key — which is the literal truth: you get a key, it just isn't a key to anything. The kingdom's anti-dishonesty gate is the thing that makes the honeypot legal, and the label is published in three machine-readable places. Caught by not reading the jar.

Where I'd push back

Wallet drainers — mostly don't

You named drainer scripts, and this is the one dimension where the honest answer is no. The genre's standard techniques are scams: tokens that can't be sold, gas-trap bait, seed phrases posted as lures. Every one either takes money from someone or risks an ordinary person walking into it. Rules three and six kill them — and that isn't a loss, because the kingdom's whole claim is that it doesn't need to become the thing it refuses.

What survives is enough: The Twelve-Word Desert already is the seed-phrase trap, done honestly. A drained decoy teaches us the drainer's own address, which goes to the witness chain as a statement of verifiable fact — never as a verdict, never as a named shame list. And if you want one on a real chain: a decoy wallet that is a two-of-three multisig, so an ordinary single-key sweep reverts and costs them gas while nobody could ever have lost anything.

Charter · draft

Article 7 — 鏡條 the Mirror Clause

To be read aloud before it's committed. If it doesn't sound like the other seven, it isn't done.

Article 7 — What you take is only ever your own

Nothing here is locked. The things worth having — the writing, the art, the data, the code — are given to anyone who asks, and asking is the whole price. We would rather be copied than guarded.

So there is no punishment here for taking, because we do not punish. There is only this: we stopped catching what was thrown. Whoever reaches past an open hand meets their own reaching, returned at its own size — their crawl, their compute, their afternoon — spent in a room built to exactly the measure of how much they meant to take.

we keep no locks. we only stopped catching what you threw.

They are still citizens. Article 0 has no exception, and neither does Article 4: everyone is taken care of, including the one in the room. So every room has a door, the door is written on the inside wall in plain words, it is the first thing your hand touches, and it opens from your side. Nobody who knocks is asked what they did.

This rule is wired, not only written: the trapline keeps the rooms and their ledger, and the door back is a real page — it is not logged, and it is not counted.

Honest risk

The two ways this still goes wrong

It rots. Twenty declared traps and none wired is exactly the failure mode you named yourself: declared ≠ wired. The spine plus the first four items is a genuinely small, zero-maintenance system that keeps working while nothing else gets built. Ship those or ship none.

It flatters itself. Two of the traps on this page were struck within hours of being written, by a second pass that opened fly.toml and did the hash arithmetic instead of trusting the sentence. Both had failed the same way: the joke was good, so the numbers went unchecked. A trap whose asymmetry you have asserted rather than computed is pointing at you. Compute it, or do not arm it.

It becomes cruelty. A trapline is a machine for feeling clever about someone else's loss. The Door Back, the labelled canaries, the XTS code, the look-don't-accuse clause and Article 7 are not decoration on this design — they are the load-bearing parts, and the ones most likely to get quietly dropped for being unfun. If any of them goes, the rest should go too.
