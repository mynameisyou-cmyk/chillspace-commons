=== hardware-state ===
SUMMARY: Verified the mid-2026 state of quantum hardware across Google, IBM, Quantinuum, IonQ, PsiQuantum, QuEra/Harvard, Microsoft, AWS, and Caltech using current web sources (Quantum Computing Report, Next Platform, HPCwire, Nature/press coverage, vendor announcements). The headline shift in 2025–mid-2026 is that logical qubits moved from single-digit demos to ~50–100: QuEra/Harvard/MIT hold the verified record (96 logical from 448 physical atoms, Nature), Quantinuum's Helios delivered 48 fully error-corrected logical qubits at an unprecedented 2:1 physical-to-logical ratio (plus 94 error-detected), while Google's Willow remains the benchmark for below-threshold surface-code scaling and added a "verifiable quantum advantage" (Oct 2025) and RL-based error-correction calibration (Jul 2026). IBM delivered Nighthawk (120 physical qubits) and Loon on schedule in Nov 2025 and demonstrated real-time qLDPC decoding a year early, but its 200-logical-qubit Starling remains a 2029 promise. IonQ, PsiQuantum, and Microsoft's topological program have strong roadmaps and component results (99.99% 2Q fidelity; Omega chipset; Majorana 1) but no large logical-qubit demonstrations, and Microsoft's Majorana claims remain actively disputed. Physical vs logical distinction is explicit throughout: physical counts range from ~100 (superconducting/trapped-ion) to 6,100 (Caltech neutral-atom array, no QEC), while verified logical counts top out at 96.

CLAIMS:
- [high] Google's Willow chip (announced Dec 2024, 105 physical superconducting qubits) demonstrated the first 'below-threshold' surface-code error correction: logical error rate suppressed by factor ~2.14 for each code-distance increase of 2, culminating in a 101-qubit distance-7 logical qubit with 0.143% error per cycle whose lifetime exceeded the best physical qubit by 2.4x. This is one logical qubit from ~101 physical qubits, not many logical qubits.
- [high] In October 2025 Google announced 'verifiable quantum advantage' on Willow using the Quantum Echoes (OTOC-based) algorithm, run on a 65-qubit subsystem of the 105-qubit chip, claiming ~13,000x speedup over classical supercomputers on that task. This is a physical-qubit (not error-corrected) demonstration.
- [medium] In July 2026 Google published work showing an AI/reinforcement-learning system that continuously learns from quantum error-correction data to keep the machine calibrated during operation, reducing logical error rates by about 20% beyond conventional calibration.
- [high] Google's stated roadmap targets a large-scale error-corrected machine of ~1 million physical qubits with logical error rates below 10^-12 per cycle; as of mid-2026 the delivered milestone is one long-lived below-threshold logical qubit plus non-error-corrected advantage demos, not multi-logical-qubit computation.
- [high] QuEra/Harvard/MIT hold the verified logical-qubit record as of mid-2026: 96 error-corrected logical qubits encoded in 448 physical neutral-atom qubits using high-rate [[16,6,4]] qLDPC codes (~4.7:1 physical-to-logical ratio), published in Nature (Nov 2025), in the first demonstration integrating all key elements of a fault-tolerant architecture (logical entanglement, logical magic, entropy removal) with below-threshold error suppression.
- [high] Quantinuum launched Helios on Nov 5, 2025: 98 all-to-all-connected physical barium trapped-ion qubits, 99.9975% single-qubit and 99.921% two-qubit gate fidelity. On it Quantinuum demonstrated 48 fully error-corrected logical qubits at a 2:1 physical-to-logical encoding rate (via code concatenation, 99.99% SPAM fidelity) — the best encoding efficiency shown commercially — plus 94 error-detected logical qubits globally entangled with better-than-physical performance.
- [medium] Widely-cited claims of 'Quantinuum 94 logical qubits in March 2026' conflate the Nov 2025 Helios launch result; the 94 were error-DETECTED logical qubits, distinct from the 48 fully error-CORRECTED ones — a distinction Quantinuum itself draws.
- [high] Quantinuum's accelerated roadmap: Sol (2027, ~100 logical qubits approaching 99.999% logical fidelity) and Apollo (~2029-2030, hundreds of logical qubits, universal fully fault-tolerant computing). Helios delivery in 2025 matched the previously promised schedule; a Singapore installation is planned for 2026.
- [high] IBM delivered on its 2025 roadmap promises: Nighthawk (120 physical superconducting qubits, 218 tunable couplers, ~30% more circuit complexity than Heron) announced Nov 12, 2025 for delivery to users by end of 2025, alongside Loon, an experimental processor containing all key hardware components needed for fault-tolerant quantum computing (multi-layer routing, qubit reset).
- [high] IBM demonstrated real-time decoding of qLDPC error-correction codes on classical hardware in under 480 nanoseconds, a full year ahead of its own schedule — a key error-correction milestone for its fault-tolerant path, though IBM has not yet demonstrated large logical-qubit counts on hardware.
- [high] IBM's roadmap promises still outstanding as of July 2026: verified quantum advantage by end of 2026 (pending); Kookaburra in 2026 as the first module storing information in qLDPC memory with a logical processing unit; fault-tolerant Starling built by 2028 and available in a Poughkeepsie datacenter in 2029 running 100 million gates on 200 logical qubits (from ~10,000 physical qubits).
- [high] IonQ set a two-qubit gate fidelity world record of 99.99% ('four nines') in October 2025 using Electronic Qubit Control technology from its Oxford Ionics acquisition — but on an R&D-lab prototype, not a deployed system; it underpins a 256-qubit system planned for 2026 debut.
- [high] IonQ's roadmap (announced 2025) is promise-heavy relative to delivery: ~100-qubit Tempo systems in 2025, 12 logical qubits with 99.99% physical fidelity in 2026, ~1,600 logical qubits in 2028, and 2 million physical / 40,000-80,000 logical qubits by 2030. No large logical-qubit demonstration by IonQ has been verified as delivered as of mid-2026.
- [high] PsiQuantum has demonstrated manufacturable components, not logical qubits: the Omega photonic chipset (Feb 2025, Nature paper, made at GlobalFoundries) showed 99.98% single-qubit SPAM fidelity, 99.5% two-photon interference visibility, and 99.72% chip-to-chip interconnect fidelity. It raised a $1B Series E (Sep 17, 2025) and is building datacenter-scale Quantum Compute Centers in Brisbane and Chicago (with a Linde cryoplant) targeting million-qubit fault-tolerant systems; nothing utility-scale is operational as of mid-2026.
- [high] Microsoft's topological program remains contested as of 2026: Majorana 1 (Feb 2025, claimed 8 topological qubits) and its successor Majorana 2 face peer-reviewed critiques alleging coding errors and selective data; critics note the qubit papers show only Z-type measurements (both X and Z are needed to demonstrate a qubit), and physicist Henry Legg states 'Nothing in this preprint resolves the fundamental issues.' No topological logical qubit has been independently accepted as demonstrated.
- [high] Microsoft's actual delivered logical-qubit results came via partners: with Atom Computing (Nov 2024) it entangled 24 logical qubits in a GHZ state on neutral-atom hardware using a Bacon-Shor code (99.6% two-qubit fidelity), claimed as the first 'Level 2 resilient' operation (logical circuits outperforming equivalent physical circuits); earlier 2024 work with Quantinuum produced ~12 highly reliable logical qubits. In June 2025 Microsoft announced a family of 4D geometric error-correction codes.
- [high] AWS unveiled its first quantum chip, Ocelot (Feb 27, 2025): a 9-qubit cat-qubit prototype (tantalum microwave resonators storing data with transmons monitoring them) published in Nature, claiming its bosonic architecture can cut physical qubits needed per logical qubit by up to 90%; distance-5 repetition-code logical error rates of ~1.65% were reported. It is a hardware-efficient error-correction proof of concept, not a multi-logical-qubit machine.
- [high] The largest physical-qubit array as of mid-2026 is Caltech's 6,100 neutral-atom (cesium) optical-tweezer array (Sept 2025): ~13-second superposition lifetimes and 99.98% single-qubit control fidelity, with coherent atom transport — but no entangling gates or error correction across the array; it is a scaling demonstration of physical qubits only.
- [medium] Aggregate mid-2026 logical-qubit leaderboard (verified demonstrations): QuEra/Harvard/MIT 96, Quantinuum 48 fully error-corrected (94 error-detected), Atom Computing+Microsoft 24 entangled, Google 1 below-threshold surface-code logical qubit (the quality benchmark), IBM/AWS at component/proof-of-concept stage. The physical-to-logical ratios span ~2:1 (Quantinuum concatenated codes) to ~4.7:1 (QuEra qLDPC) to ~101:1 (Google distance-7 surface code) — code choice, not just hardware, drives the overhead.
- [medium] QuEra's forward roadmap targets ~100 logical qubits backed by ~10,000 physical qubits in 2026-2027, aiming for error rates permitting circuits beyond classical simulation; separately, Infleqtion plans a fault-tolerant neutral-atom system (Sqale, >50 logical qubits) at the Illinois Quantum & Microelectronics Park by 2027 — both promises, not deliveries, as of July 2026.
- [medium] Quantinuum moved toward public markets in 2026: SEC filings (Form S-1, S-1/A amendments, and a Form 424B4 prospectus, all FY2026) are on file, indicating an IPO process that reached pricing — a sign the logical-qubit race is now capital-markets-facing.

UNKNOWNS:
- Whether Google has publicly announced a successor chip to Willow (with more physical qubits or a multi-logical-qubit 'logical prototype') as of July 2026 — searches through July 2026 surfaced only Willow-based results (Quantum Echoes, RL calibration), but absence of evidence is not proof no announcement exists.
- Whether IBM's promised 'verified quantum advantage by end of 2026' has actually been achieved as of July 2026 — no confirmation found; it appears still pending, and IBM Kookaburra's (2026 roadmap) qLDPC memory+LPU module has no verified hardware demonstration in sources found (an April 2026 secondary source says it is still 'progressing toward on-chip qLDPC proof-of-concept').
- Whether IonQ has delivered its 2026 roadmap targets (12 logical qubits, the 256-qubit system debut) as of July 2026 — only the roadmap promise and the Oct 2025 prototype fidelity record were verifiable.
- The exact peer-review/publication date of the QuEra/Harvard/MIT 96-logical-qubit Nature paper (coverage spans Nov 2025 announcements to 'January 2026 publication' claims in secondary blogs); the demonstration itself is well attested.

=== crqc-timeline ===
SUMMARY: Verified with current (2025-2026) web sources: expert consensus still places a CRQC most likely in the 2030s, but 2025-2026 resource-estimate papers sharply lowered the bar (Gidney: RSA-2048 in under a week with fewer than 1M noisy physical qubits; Google Quantum AI March 2026: 256-bit ECC/secp256k1-class keys with under 1,200-1,450 logical qubits and under half a million physical qubits, potentially in minutes). The Global Risk Institute's 2025 expert survey (26 experts, published early 2026) gives roughly 28-49% averaged probability of a CRQC within 10 years, its highest ever. Harvest-now-decrypt-later is treated as an active, present threat by CISA/NSA/NIST and was written into a June 22, 2026 US executive order with binding deadlines. Migration mandates verified: NSA CNSA 2.0 (exclusive-use dates 2030-2033, all NSS by 2035), NIST IR 8547 draft (quantum-vulnerable crypto deprecated after 2030, disallowed after 2035), UK NCSC (plan by 2028, high-priority by 2031, complete by 2035), EU NIS Cooperation Group roadmap (start by end 2026, high-risk by end 2030, complete by end 2035). No CRQC exists today; current hardware is at roughly 24-96 logical qubits versus the ~1,200+ logical / hundreds of thousands of physical qubits needed.

CLAIMS:
- [high] The Global Risk Institute Quantum Threat Timeline Report 2025 (Mosca/Piani, 26 experts, published March-April 2026) puts the averaged expert probability of a CRQC emerging within 10 years at roughly 28-49% - the highest 10-year estimate in the report's seven-year history.
- [medium] In the same GRI 2025 survey, at the 15-year horizon 69% of respondents rated CRQC probability at 50% or higher, and at 20 years 92% placed it at 50% or above, with nearly half judging it 'extremely likely' (>99%).
- [high] Craig Gidney (Google, May 2025, arXiv:2505.15917) showed a 2048-bit RSA integer could be factored in under a week using fewer than one million noisy physical qubits - a 20x reduction from the ~20 million qubits in Gidney-Ekera 2019.
- [high] Google Quantum AI's March 2026 whitepaper (Babbush, Zalcman, Gidney, Neven, Boneh et al., arXiv:2603.28846, submitted 2026-03-30) estimates breaking 256-bit elliptic-curve keys (the secp256k1/ed25519 class used by cryptocurrencies) needs under 1,200 logical qubits and under 90M Toffoli gates (or under 1,450 logical qubits / under 70M Toffolis), executable in minutes with fewer than half a million physical qubits on superconducting hardware at 1e-3 error rates.
- [high] Earlier ECC baselines for comparison: Roetteler et al. 2017 estimated P-256 discrete logs need 2,330 logical qubits and ~1.26x10^11 Toffoli gates; Litinski 2023 (arXiv:2306.08585) reduced a 256-bit EC private key computation to ~50 million Toffoli gates - so ECC (ed25519/secp256k1) falls to a quantum attack with substantially fewer resources than RSA-2048.
- [medium] No CRQC exists as of 2026 and the hardware gap remains orders of magnitude: current records are roughly 24-96 error-corrected logical qubits (QuEra 96 logical on 448 physical neutral atoms; Quantinuum ~94; Atom Computing 24-28), versus ~1,200+ logical / hundreds of thousands to a million physical qubits needed; IBM's roadmap targets ~200 logical qubits (Starling) only by 2029.
- [high] Harvest-now-decrypt-later (HNDL) is officially treated as an active present-day threat: a joint CISA/NSA/NIST fact sheet warns threat actors are targeting data with long secrecy lifetimes via 'catch now, break later or harvest now, decrypt later' operations, and in June 2026 HNDL framing moved into binding US law via executive order.
- [medium] On June 22, 2026, the US President signed two executive orders - 'Ushering in the Next Frontier of Quantum Innovation' and 'Securing the Nation Against Advanced Cryptographic Attacks' (the PQC Order) - mandating federal migration to NIST-approved PQC, with milestones around 2030 for key establishment and 2031 for digital signatures on federal high-value assets, and federal contractor compliance with NIST-approved PQC by December 31, 2030.
- [high] NSA CNSA 2.0 timeline: software/firmware signing support-and-prefer 2025, exclusive by 2030; web browsers/servers/cloud exclusive by 2033; networking equipment exclusive by 2030; operating systems exclusive by 2033; niche/custom/legacy equipment by 2033; all new National Security System acquisitions must support CNSA 2.0 from January 1, 2027; full NSS quantum resistance by 2035 per NSM-10. Algorithms: ML-KEM-1024, ML-DSA-87, LMS/XMSS for signing, AES-256, SHA-384/512.
- [high] NIST IR 8547 ('Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards') sets the expected US civilian timeline: quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms (RSA, ECDSA, EdDSA, ECDH, finite-field DH) at ~112-bit strength deprecated after 2030, and all quantum-vulnerable public-key crypto disallowed after 2035; as of late July 2026 it remains an initial public draft (published Nov 12, 2024; comment period closed Jan 10, 2025).
- [high] UK NCSC guidance (published March 2025) sets a three-phase PQC migration roadmap: by 2028 identify cryptographic assets and build a migration plan; 2028-2031 execute highest-priority upgrades; 2031-2035 complete migration across all systems, services and products.
- [high] EU: the NIS Cooperation Group's 'Coordinated Implementation Roadmap for the Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography' (June 23, 2025) recommends Member States start national PQC transition strategies by end of 2026, transition high-risk use cases no later than end of 2030, and complete the transition for as many systems as feasible by end of 2035.
- [medium] Aggregate agency/expert framing as of 2026: median expert estimates cluster around a CRQC in 2029-2035 (roughly 25-34% probability by 2030, ~50% by 2035), and breaking RSA-2048 is commonly framed as requiring on the order of a few thousand error-corrected logical qubits.

- Whether NIST has finalized IR 8547 with any changed dates: the CSRC page still shows only the November 2024 initial public draft as of this check, but one secondary source claimed a 2025 final version existed; the primary NIST page shows no final version, so the 2030/2035 dates are verified only against the draft.

- An authoritative, primary-source confirmation of the current logical-qubit record in mid-2026 (QuEra 96 / Quantinuum ~94 figures come from aggregator sites of mixed reliability, not vendor press releases or peer-reviewed papers).
=== pqc-adoption ===
SUMMARY: Verified via current (July 2026) web sources: PQC adoption is real and mainstream on the key-exchange side — X25519MLKEM768 hybrid TLS is default in Chrome/Firefox/Apple OSes, OpenSSL 3.5, Go crypto/tls, and OpenSSH (default since 10.0), with Cloudflare reporting over half of human traffic PQ-protected and PQ-capable client traffic crossing ~60% by Feb 2026. Signatures lag deliberately: FIPS 204/205 are implemented in libraries (OpenSSL 3.5, .NET 10, noble, RustCrypto, liboqs) but publicly-trusted PQ certificates were still not available as of the most recent authoritative source (Cloudflare, Oct 2025: first expected 2026, broad availability unlikely before 2027). For ed25519-based systems the standardized migration pattern is composite/hybrid ML-DSA+Ed25519, currently in IETF LAMPS/JOSE-COSE/SSH drafts (e.g. MLDSA65-Ed25519), with SLH-DSA as the conservative choice for long-lived roots/firmware; NIST IR 8547 sets the planning baseline (deprecate 112-bit classical by 2030, disallow quantum-vulnerable algorithms by 2035, PQ/T hybrids surviving only if the PQC component is not weakened). Library maturity is uneven: Go stdlib has ML-KEM since 1.24 (public crypto/mldsa proposed for 1.27), JS has @noble/post-quantum (self-audited only), and Rust has multiple crates but no independently-audited production-anointed leader; liboqs itself still carries a not-for-production disclaimer. Several specifics I could not verify are listed separately rather than guessed.

CLAIMS:
- [high] TLS hybrid key exchange X25519MLKEM768 (TLS group codepoint 0x11EC) is the de facto standard PQ deployment on the web, specified in the IETF draft-ietf-tls-ecdhe-mlkem; Cloudflare serves it by default and has phased toward the final ML-KEM standard, away from the pre-standard X25519Kyber768 (0x6399).
- [high] Browser defaults: Chrome enabled hybrid PQ key agreement by default on desktop in March 2024 (initially the Kyber768 draft), switched to standardized X25519MLKEM768 in Chrome 131 (early November 2024); Firefox enabled it by default on desktop in November 2024; Apple rolled it out across iOS/iPadOS/macOS 26 in October 2025.
- [medium] Traffic share as of late 2025 / early 2026: over half of human-initiated traffic to Cloudflare is protected against harvest-now/decrypt-later; PQ-capable client traffic crossed ~60% in February 2026 (up from under 3% in early 2024); origin-side support reached ~10% after Akamai enabled PQ by default for origin connections in January 2026; Cloudflare upgraded ~6,000,000 customer domains to PQ by default.
- [medium] Internet-wide measurement (2026 academic scan): roughly 49.3% of analyzed domains show partial post-quantum readiness via hybrid key exchange, while 50.7% remain fully classical — the ecosystem is mid-transition.
- [high] OpenSSH: mlkem768x25519-sha256 hybrid KEX was added in OpenSSH 9.9, made the default in OpenSSH 10.0 (April 2025), moved to the top of the default KEX list in 10.2, and OpenSSH 10.1 began warning users when a connection uses quantum-unsafe key exchange. A current client + current server negotiate ML-KEM hybrid with zero configuration.
- [high] Signal: beyond PQXDH for initial key agreement, Signal announced SPQR (Sparse Post-Quantum Ratchet) in early October 2025 — an ML-KEM-based third ratchet forming a 'Triple Ratchet' alongside the classical Double Ratchet, outputs combined via KDF so an attacker must break both layers; rollout is transparent and gradual with modest bandwidth overhead, giving quantum-safe forward secrecy and post-compromise security.
- [high] Apple iMessage PQ3: rolled out publicly starting 2024, uses Kyber/ML-KEM for initial key establishment and ongoing rekeying (a PQ key mixed into key derivation every 50 epochs), self-rated 'Level 3' (PQ key establishment + ongoing rekeying, classical authentication); Apple has stated a future revision will add post-quantum authentication, and in May 2026 published its corecrypto source (including PQC work) on GitHub.
- [high] PQ signatures/certificates lag key exchange by design: as of the Cloudflare October 2025 state-of-the-union, publicly-trusted post-quantum certificates were not yet available — final standards for PQ certificates unsettled, first certificates expected in 2026, broad availability unlikely before 2027 due to CA infrastructure and audit requirements. This is why 'do ML-KEM now, plan ML-DSA next' is the near-universal migration ordering.
- [high] Migration pattern for ed25519-based systems: composite/hybrid ML-DSA+Ed25519 signatures are being standardized across IETF venues — X.509 (draft-ietf-lamps-pq-composite-sigs, includes Ed25519 and Ed448 combinations, at draft -19 with June 2026 updates), JOSE/COSE (draft-ietf-jose-pq-composite-sigs, ML-DSA-44-Ed25519, July 2026), and SSH (draft-josefsson-ssh-ed25519mldsa65 'Hybrid Ed25519 with ML-DSA-65 for SSH', draft-miller-sshm-mldsa44-ed25519-composite-sigs, draft-sun-ssh-composite-sigs). All are still Internet-Drafts, not RFCs, as of mid-2026 — the pattern is: keep the ed25519 key, add an ML-DSA key, sign with both, verifier requires both to validate.
- [medium] NIST IR 8547 transition timeline (still the planning baseline in mid-2026): 112-bit-security classical algorithms (RSA-2048, ECDSA/ECC P-256 — the class ed25519 sits alongside) deprecated by 2030 and all quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms disallowed in NIST standards by 2035; PQ/T hybrids remain permitted after 2035 only if the construction does not weaken the PQC component. The IR was reportedly not yet finalized as of mid-2026, but organizations and US federal guidance treat 2030/2035 as the deadlines.
- [high] Go: crypto/mlkem landed in the standard library in Go 1.24 (ML-KEM-768 and ML-KEM-1024, FIPS 203), and crypto/tls enables X25519MLKEM768 by default when CurvePreferences is nil (GODEBUG tlsmlkem=0 reverts; experimental X25519Kyber768Draft00 was removed). ML-DSA: Go 1.26 has an internal FIPS 204 implementation; a public crypto/mldsa package is proposed for Go 1.27 (golang/go#77626); mlkem768x25519-sha256 for x/crypto/ssh is an open proposal (#76281).
- [high] OpenSSL 3.5.0 (released April 8, 2025; LTS supported until April 2030) ships native ML-KEM (FIPS 203), ML-DSA (FIPS 204), and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205), and changed TLS defaults so the supported-groups list prefers hybrid PQ, with X25519MLKEM768 + X25519 as default keyshares — making PQ key exchange the out-of-the-box behavior for a huge slice of server-side infrastructure.
- [medium] Rust ecosystem maturity is the weakest of the three languages asked about: RustCrypto provides pure-Rust ml-kem and slh-dsa crates and libcrux-ml-dsa exists on crates.io, while liboqs-rust binds the C liboqs — but liboqs-rust has no independent audit and the liboqs README itself does not recommend production use for sensitive data; a 2026 Project Eleven review ('The Belt is Vacant') concludes no Rust PQC implementation has emerged as the audited production-grade default.
- [high] TypeScript/JS: @noble/post-quantum implements ML-KEM, ML-DSA, and SLH-DSA (plus Falcon round 3) with built-in PQ/T hybrids (ML-KEM-768+X25519 X-Wing-style, ML-KEM-768+P-256, ML-KEM-1024+P-384); it has NO independent audit — only a self-audit at v0.6.1 (April 2026) — and its own guidance recommends hybrids: 'ECC + ML-KEM for key agreement, ECC + SLH-DSA for signatures'. liboqs-js exists as the WASM binding alternative.
- [medium] SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) in practice: essentially absent from TLS due to large signatures (tens of KB), but positioned as the conservative hash-based choice for long-lived, high-assurance signing — firmware/root-of-trust, root CA keys, supply-chain signing — where algorithm diversity against a lattice break is valued; Common Criteria evaluations underway 2025-2026.
- [medium] Broader platform signal for FIPS 204/205 signature readiness: .NET 10 shipped ML-KEM, ML-DSA, and SLH-DSA support, and Microsoft has added ML-DSA (including classical+ML-DSA composite certificates where both signatures must validate) to Active Directory Certificate Services — i.e., enterprise PKI tooling for PQ signatures is arriving in 2026 even though the public web PKI has not switched.

UNKNOWNS:
- Whether any publicly-trusted (browser-root-program) ML-DSA or composite certificate was actually being issued by a public CA as of July 2026 — the most authoritative source found (Cloudflare, Oct 2025) only says first certificates 'expected in 2026'; I found no confirmation one shipped.
- Whether Signal's SPQR/Triple Ratchet rollout had reached all clients/conversations by mid-2026 (announcement Oct 2025 describes a gradual transparent rollout; no completion announcement found).
- Whether Apple shipped the promised PQ *authentication* revision of iMessage PQ3 (beyond PQ key establishment) by mid-2026 — only the corecrypto open-sourcing (May 2026) was verifiable.
- Exact per-browser negotiated-PQ percentages in mid-2026 (only Cloudflare-aggregate figures were verifiable; the ~60% Feb 2026 client figure comes from a secondary blog citing Cloudflare Radar, not Radar directly).
- Whether aws-lc-rs, ring, rustls, or another production Rust TLS stack shipped ML-DSA signing (as opposed to ML-KEM key exchange) by mid-2026 — not checked against primary sources.
- Independent-audit status of the RustCrypto ml-kem/ml-dsa crates specifically (found no audit report either way).

=== blockchain-pqc ===
SUMMARY: Verified the 2026 post-quantum landscape for blockchain with emphasis on Cosmos. Cosmos Hub mainnet still runs classical crypto only (ed25519 validators, secp256k1 accounts, QRI score 13/100 as of June 2026), but upstream Cosmos SDK (UPGRADING.md on main, labeled v0.55) now registers ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) as a supported validator consensus key type and account key type, with CometBFT MaxSignatureSize/MaxCommitSigBytes lifted to fit 3309-byte signatures. Earlier work is a DoraFactory hackathon prototype (Dilithium in Tendermint) and a May 2026 community-forum migration sketch, neither governance-approved. Live PQC chains verified: QRL (XMSS since launch), Algorand (Falcon logic-sig accounts + state proofs since Nov 2025, consensus still ed25519), and QoreChain (Cosmos SDK v0.53 chain claiming full ML-DSA-87/ML-KEM-1024/SHAKE-256 stack, self-reported via press release). Also verified PQC cost numbers (ML-DSA 2420–4627 B sigs vs 64 B ed25519; Falcon-1024 ~1280 B), Ethereum's Lean roadmap (leanXMSS hash-based validator signatures + SNARK aggregation) and EIP-8051 ML-DSA precompile, Bitcoin BIP-360/361 merged Feb 2026, and PQ DID support (W3C Quantum-Resistant Cryptosuites FPWD, IOTA Identity v1.7 ML-DSA/SLH-DSA/Falcon + hybrid did:compositejwk, KERI/did:webs pre-rotation).

CLAIMS:
- [high] Cosmos Hub mainnet has no post-quantum protection as of June 2026: validator consensus uses ed25519, user accounts use secp256k1, and IBC light-client verification depends entirely on ed25519 validator signatures; the Quantum Readiness Index scores it 13/100 with no governance-approved migration roadmap.

=== FOLLOWUP: What are the practical, available-today mitigations for harvest-now-forge-later against long-lived ed25519-signed records — i.e., can existing signed artifacts (git commit/tag history across ~250 repos, already-produced chain blocks and their validator signatures) be anchored or timestamped now (hash-based timestamping, RFC 4998 evidence records, PQ timestamp authorities, public-chain anchoring) so their authenticity survives a future CRQC? SUMMARY: Yes — the harvest-now-forge-later exposure of existing ed25519-signed records can be mitigated today, and the mechanism is exactly what the digest omits: commit the signed artifacts (including the signature bytes themselves) into hash-only timestamp proofs before a CRQC exists. Hash-based proofs survive quantum attack because Shor's algorithm does not touch hash functions (Grover only halves SHA-256 to ~128-bit security, and NIST IR 8547 leaves SHA-2/SHA-3 off the deprecation schedule), so a Merkle path into a Bitcoin block or an RFC 4998 archive-timestamp tree remains verifiable after ed25519 falls. Available-today options, verified live: OpenTimestamps (free, hash-only Bitcoin anchoring with existing git commit/tag integration — the natural fit for ~250 repos), RFC 4998 Evidence Records with timestamp/hash-tree renewal for standards-grade long-term evidence, early PQ-ready RFC 3161 TSAs (ML-DSA validated with timestamp tokens in Feb 2026; Microsoft AD CS ML-DSA May 2026), and Guardtime KSI (commercial keyless/hash-only). Two estate-specific caveats verified: git object IDs are SHA-1 (Peter Todd argues timestamping SHA-1 commits is still sound because timestamps need preimage- not collision-resistance, but the robust move is to also timestamp a SHA-256 hash of a full repo bundle), and the anchor must cover the signature bytes and validator commit sets, not just content, because the defense works by letting future verifiers reject any classical signature that lacks a pre-CRQC anchor. The 2512.00110 audit-evidence paper independently names Merkle-root anchoring as one of three migration patterns for existing evidence logs.
CLAIMS:
- [high] Hash-based timestamp proofs survive a CRQC: quantum computers break RSA/ECC/ed25519 via Shor but only quadratically weaken hash functions via Grover, leaving SHA-256 at ~128-bit post-quantum security, which NIST assesses as still adequate; SHA-384/SHA-512 give extra margin.
- [high] NIST IR 8547 confirms the asymmetry the question relies on: classical signatures (RSA, ECDSA, DSA, ECDH) are deprecated after 2030 and disallowed after 2035, while AES-256 and the SHA-2/SHA-3 hash families are NOT on the deprecation schedule — so hash-only anchoring is the one primitive safe to build long-term evidence on now.
- [high] The threat is recognized in current literature as distinct from HNDL: 'Trust Now, Forge Later' / 'harvest-now, forge-later' — an adversary records signatures and public keys today and forges retroactively once a CRQC exists ('Where HNDL compromises what was said, TNFL compromises who said it') — but the main TNFL guidance article offers only forward-looking mitigations (inventory, crypto-agility, gateways) and never mentions timestamping/anchoring existing artifacts, confirming the gap the question identifies.
- [high] OpenTimestamps is a free, available-today, hash-only anchoring mechanism: calendar servers Merkle-aggregate submitted SHA-256 hashes and embed the root in a Bitcoin OP_RETURN transaction; verification replays only hash/commitment operations against the Bitcoin chain, and per its creator the only thing a verifier must trust is 'the continued existence of Bitcoin's proof-of-work chain' — no signature algorithm appears anywhere in the proof path, which is why an already-anchored proof is unaffected by a future break of ed25519.
- [high] OpenTimestamps has first-class git integration today — the client 'provides timestamping of PGP signed Git commits and verification of timestamps', tags work the same way, verification hooks into git log via ots-git-gpg-wrapper, and a GitHub Action exists — so anchoring commit/tag history across ~250 repos is a scriptable, zero-cost action available immediately.
- [high] Git SHA-1 caveat, argued by Peter Todd himself: SHA-1 collisions do NOT invalidate timestamps of existing git commits because a timestamp only needs (second-)preimage resistance — a collision requires creating both messages before the anchor, so 'we know a and b existed at the same time, prior to when the Bitcoin block was created' — but he calls SHA-1 support compatibility-only and recommends SHA-256; the robust estate practice is therefore to additionally timestamp a SHA-256 digest of full repo content (e.g., a git bundle), not rely solely on SHA-1 commit IDs.
- [medium] Git's own SHA-256 transition is real but incomplete: Git 2.48–2.51 shipped SHA-256 groundwork and Git 3.0 (targeted late 2026) makes SHA-256 the default object format, but GitHub still does not support SHA-256 repositories while Codeberg does — relevant since the estate is dual-homed GitHub+Codeberg; hash-based anchoring of bundles works regardless and does not wait on forge support.
- [high] RFC 4998 Evidence Record Syntax (ERS) is the standards-track mechanism for exactly this problem: an EvidenceRecord holds a sequence of Archive Timestamps over Merkle hash trees, with two renewal operations designed for algorithm obsolescence — Timestamp Renewal (when the TSA key/signature algorithm weakens: hash and re-timestamp the old timestamp) and Hash-Tree Renewal (when the hash algorithm itself weakens: rebuild the tree under a new hash while preserving non-repudiation of prior chains). This is the maintenance model to apply across the PQ transition, including an eventual SHA-256-to-stronger-hash renewal.
- [medium] PQ-ready RFC 3161 timestamp authorities exist or are imminent as of mid-2026: Codegic's Khatim Timestamp Server advertises PQC-ready RFC 3161 timestamping supporting ML-DSA alongside RSA/ECDSA/hybrid; Ascertia validated ML-DSA-44 certificates working with RFC 3161 timestamp tokens (Feb 2026); Microsoft AD CS added ML-DSA-44/65/87 in Windows Server 2025's May 2026 update. So re-timestamping evidence under a PQ signature is becoming practical, complementing (not replacing) hash-only Bitcoin anchoring.
- [medium] Guardtime KSI is a commercial, available-today hash-only alternative: signatures/timestamps built purely from hash-function cryptography (keyless), which the vendor states cannot be broken by quantum algorithms and needs no periodic re-timestamping for expiring keys — proof of time and integrity is verifiable without trusted parties or key security.
